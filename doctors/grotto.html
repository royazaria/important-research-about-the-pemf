Integrating PEMF Technology in Israeli Health Institutions: Summary

Background

PEMF (Pulsed Electromagnetic Field Therapy) technology uses low-frequency pulsed magnetic fields for therapeutic purposes, mainly in pain management, orthopedic and neurological rehabilitation, fracture healing and the reduction of inflammation.

Editor's note: Prof. Grotto's original continues with a sentence on the technology's FDA history and its use at US institutions. We have replaced it with the precise version: since 1979, electromagnetic bone-growth stimulators from other manufacturers have held FDA approval for fracture healing, and today some devices hold 510(k) clearance for pain and edema. Regulatory approval belongs to a specific model and indication, not to the technology as a whole.

Biological Mechanism of Action

PEMF acts at the cellular and molecular level on ion channels, mitochondrial activity, the release of growth factors (IGF-1, VEGF, BMP), the stimulation of ATP production and the reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines.

Clinical Indications

  • Fractures and orthopedic fixation: faster healing of fractures with poor union (non-union) and improved fixation stability after surgery (fusion).
  • Osteoarthritis (OA): pain reduction and functional improvement in the joints, especially the knee and hip.
  • Low back pain: reduction of chronic pain after surgery and in cases of Failed Back Surgery Syndrome.
  • Fibromyalgia: improvement in functional measures and sleep quality, and reduced inflammation.
  • Diabetic and chronic wounds: faster healing and fewer infections.
  • Neurological rehabilitation: improved nerve conduction, motor function and balance in post-stroke rehabilitation.
  • Treatment of chronic pain in older adults.

In comparative studies with similar technologies such as TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation) and ultrasound, PEMF technology demonstrated superior effectiveness in treating chronic pain and accelerating rehabilitation.

Safety and Regulation

  • FDA Class III device approval, with indications for fracture healing, soft-tissue pain and osteoarthritis.
  • CE marking and AMAR approval in Israel for use as part of physiotherapy treatment.
  • Negligible side effects, with no heat generation or significant biological risk. Main contraindications: pacemaker, pregnancy and active tumors.
  • In use in Israel in dozens of private clinics.

Editor's note: the FDA and CE approvals mentioned here belong to specific PEMF devices from other manufacturers. The equipment PainFree supplies holds no FDA approval or clearance. In Israel, the Magnetobox model is listed in the Ministry of Health medical devices registry (AMAR No. 27370034, held by S.Z. Pharma Medical Ltd.). A registry listing is a registration, not an approval.

Health Units Where the Technology Can Be Integrated

  • Orthopedics
  • Rehabilitation
  • Physiotherapy units
  • Geriatric institutions
  • Assisted living

Practical Advantages for Health Institutions

  • Integrating a new technology that can serve as a model for delivering innovative physiotherapy.
  • Reduced load on physiotherapy services.
  • The option of self-administered treatment by the patient (no therapist needed at the patient's side).
  • For some indications, the option of home treatment, which shortens waiting lists.
  • Shorter hospital stays.
  • Reduced use of medication (mainly painkillers).
  • An addition to community-based or post-surgical rehabilitation.

The original also sets out a proposed economic model for Israeli health institutions (device rental and per-treatment pricing in shekels). It is left out of this translation because it applies only to Israel.

Summary and Recommendation

The scientific evidence, the regulatory approval and the clear economic savings point to a high potential for integrating PEMF as an integral part of treatment and rehabilitation programs in Israeli health institutions. It is recommended to start with an implementation pilot and to evaluate clinical and economic effectiveness under real-world conditions.
