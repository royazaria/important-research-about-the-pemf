Evidence guide

PEMF and chronic low back pain: what the trials actually show.

Chronic low back pain is the most common reason people look for a non-drug option, and the question we hear most from clinicians and clinic owners is simple: is there real evidence? Here are the three studies that matter most, what each one measured, and where each one stops.

The short answer

The largest pooled analysis to date, a 2022 meta-analysis of 14 randomized trials with 618 participants in Clinical Rehabilitation, found that pulsed electromagnetic field therapy lowered pain scores in chronic low back pain more than sham treatment. It did not find a clear difference in acute back pain, and it did not find a significant difference in physical-function scores (PubMed 35077249).

The three studies, one by one

1. Meta-analysis of 14 trials (Sun et al., Clinical Rehabilitation, 2022)

618 participants across 14 randomized trials. Compared with sham and with the other therapies it was tested against, PEMF lowered pain scores in non-specific low back pain, and the difference was significant in chronic cases. Limits: no significant benefit in acute back pain, and no significant change in physical-function measures (PubMed 35077249).

2. Systematic review of 9 trials (Kull et al., Wiener Medizinische Wochenschrift, 2025)

420 participants with non-specific low back pain. In 5 of the 9 trials, PEMF reduced pain and improved function significantly compared with the control group, and no trial reported a serious adverse event. Limits: 4 trials found no advantage, the protocols varied widely, and the review did not pool the results into a single estimate (PubMed 37999784).

3. Randomized trial in 50 patients (Elshiwi et al., Brazilian Journal of Physical Therapy, 2019)

Every participant with chronic low back pain did a physiotherapy program; half also received active PEMF and half a sham device, 12 sessions over four weeks. Disability scores improved by about 8 points more in the PEMF group, and lumbar range of motion improved more as well. Limits: the difference in pain scores alone was not clear-cut, and it was a single-center trial (PubMed 30177406).

How to read this as a clinician or clinic owner

  • The signal is in chronic, not acute, back pain. That is where the pooled data are strongest.
  • Pain scores and function scores are different outcomes. The large meta-analysis saw a difference in the first and not the second; the 50-patient trial saw it in function.
  • The trials used different devices and protocols. A result from one device and protocol does not transfer automatically to another.
  • Exclusions matter. Implanted electronic devices such as pacemakers, pregnancy and active malignancy in the area are standard exclusions. See safety and regulation.

Questions people ask

Is PEMF the same as a static magnet?

No. Static magnets produce a constant field; PEMF devices deliver timed electromagnetic pulses. The trials above studied pulsed devices.

Does it replace exercise or physiotherapy?

The 50-patient trial above combined both: every participant exercised, and the comparison was active versus sham PEMF on top of that program.
