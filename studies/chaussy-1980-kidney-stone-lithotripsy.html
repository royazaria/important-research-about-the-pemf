The first report of shockwaves breaking up kidney stones

In short

Munich researchers broke up kidney stones with shockwaves generated outside the body, first in dogs, then in patients. Fragments passed in 96% of 60 dogs and in 20 of 21 patients with stones in the kidney's collecting area; 2 patients with ureter stones still needed surgery. Side effects were slight blood in the urine and occasional colic.

Why it matters

The starting point of medical shockwave therapy; this uncontrolled Lancet case series showed stones could be broken without surgery, an approach later adapted for tendon, bone and urologic uses.
