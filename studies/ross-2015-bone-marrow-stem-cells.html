In short

This Wake Forest review gathered laboratory studies on human bone marrow stem cells, which can become bone, cartilage or fat. Studies reported that low-frequency electromagnetic fields can boost bone- and cartilage-forming development and cell growth, but results depended on frequency, intensity and exposure time, and the authors discussed how to move toward clinical trials.

Why it matters

A review of laboratory studies on cells: it describes a possible mechanism for bone and cartilage effects, not a treatment effect in people.

A plain-English summary written from the published abstract by the editors of this site. It describes this study, not any particular device or clinic, and it is not medical advice. Read the original on PubMed for the full methods and results.
